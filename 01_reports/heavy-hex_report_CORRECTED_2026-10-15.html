Reset-free syndrome extraction on IBM heavy-hex

Corrected report · 15 October 2026 · replaces the 14 October version (the original is not published)

Headline. Removing mid-circuit resets from syndrome extraction for IBM's heavy-hex subsystem code (d=3) lowered the Z-basis logical error per round by ×1.66–2.07 on ibm_kingston in four runs, every one 2σ-separated, including a clean pre-registered rerun on 15 October (×1.72, 10,000 shots). The X-basis gain is smaller (×1.13–1.42). On ibm_marrakesh the direction held but was not significant. Break-even is not reached: the logical qubit lives 0.092× (Z) and 0.136× (X) as long as the best usable physical qubit in the clean test (lifetimes defined in section 3). What changed from the 14 October report: six pre-registered predictions that failed are now shown as failures. The 14 October break-even pass was obtained post hoc and is now labelled that way; the 15 October rerun tested it cleanly. The scaling (Λ) claim is restricted to simulation.

3. Clean rerun, 15 October 2026 (Qmem3)

Design, fixed before submission: live probe of all 156 qubits (job db88jo6r6oqs7388vrs0). A qubit is unusable if its mean readout error is above 10%, either one-sided readout error is above 15%, or its T1 is not identifiable or above 2 ms. The filter excluded q113, q120, q121 and q146, and q146 was still dead (50.0% readout). Zero-SWAP placement used calibration scoring and never touched an excluded qubit. One logical arm per basis (compressed) was fixed in advance for break-even. Physical references were the 3 usable qubits with the longest probe T1 (Z) or T2 (X), idled with X–X DD for 7–400 µs. 30 circuits × 10,000 shots; 99 s of QPU time.

The calibration-fed model was again optimistic, by a factor of 1.5–2.1. The probe-fed model stayed within ±30% in all four arms. Physical lifetimes now have ±4% bands, because the waits reach 400 µs; on 14 October they stopped at ≈29 µs.

Lifetime definitions and a re-check

The logical lifetime τL is defined by 1 − 2pL(t) = A·exp(−t/τL). Physical references idled under the same X–X DD (pulses at ¼ and ¾ of each wait). Z references start in |1⟩: the qubit sits in |1⟩ only half the time, so 1 − 2P(0) decays close to exponentially, and the symmetric fit recovers T1 within about 5%. A code review raised the concern that a |1⟩ reference decays towards P(0) = 1, which would make the symmetric fit report about 0.4 T1. That is true without DD, but not for this sequence. Fitting the exact pulse-sequence model to the raw data (recompute_breakeven.py) gives T1 = 543 µs [522, 565] for q155. So the corrected Z ratio is 0.092×, against 0.096× from the pre-registered code. P18 holds and P19 fails under both.

5. What stands, what does not

  • Stands: dropping resets (flags kept in X) lowers Z-basis logical error by about ×1.7 on Kingston, replicated four times. X–X DD lowers the error (4/4 on Marrakesh, direction only; arms were near saturation). The calibration-fed model is optimistic and a seconds-long live probe fixes most of that. Break-even is far away: 0.092× (Z) and 0.136× (X) in the clean test.
  • Does not stand: the 14 October break-even "pass" (post hoc); placement and judging methods predicting their own direction (P10, P13, P15 fail); "Λ<1 on hardware" (never run at d=5); DD per-round values compared with compression ε.
  • Still open: d=5 on hardware (QPU budget); multi-day replication (hhmem2 automates it); a stronger decoder; Ramsey vs echo to explain the DD gain.

6. Verified against IBM raw data

All 9 hardware jobs were re-fetched from IBM Quantum on 15 Oct and re-analysed with independent bit-parsing and fitting code (04_hardware_runs/INDEPENDENT_REDERIVATION.json). IBM's job records confirm the backend (ibm_kingston or ibm_marrakesh), the creation time (14 Oct 19:14–20:57 EEST; 15 Oct 11:09 EEST), status DONE, and the shot count (5,000 per circuit, or 10,000 for the DD job and the 15 Oct rerun). Every ε in sections 1–4 was reproduced to two decimals, including all 8 arms of the DD-type job db7qqker (P6: 7.48%, Z tournament / staggered DD). An earlier version of the re-derivation merged the uniform-DD and staggered-DD arms of that job; this was fixed. QPU time per job (IBM-reported): 19–47 s on 14 Oct; 99 s for the rerun.

7. Reproducibility

Notebooks: Qmem2 (14 Oct record, untouched) and Qmem3 (15 Oct rerun). Code: hhmem.py and hhmem2.py, which adds the probe, dead-qubit filter, pre-registration and verdicts. Dry run (dry_run.py) on FakeKingston with an injected dead q146: the filter excluded it. Control test with the filter disabled: q146 was chosen again and P20 printed FAILS. Raw plan, probe and verdict files are in 04_hardware_runs. All data can be retrieved from IBM Quantum by job ID.
