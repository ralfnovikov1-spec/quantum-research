Reset-free syndrome extraction on IBM heavy-hex: replication, pre-registered tests and honest negative results

Research update · 15 October 2026 · Ralf Novikov · code and raw data: github.com/ralfnovikov1-spec/quantum-research · analysis prepared with AI assistance (Claude, Anthropic)

In one paragraph. On IBM's 156-qubit Heron processors, removing mid-circuit resets from heavy-hex syndrome extraction lowered the Z-basis logical error per round by about ×1.7 on ibm_kingston, in four runs, each 2σ-separated. That includes a pre-registered rerun at 10,000 shots. This independently replicates Harper et al. (2025), who showed the effect first on ibm_marrakesh. All 9 hardware jobs were re-fetched from IBM and re-derived with independent code. Two new ideas were tested on hardware under pre-registration: probe-informed decoding failed, and leakage-aware decoding failed to improve the logical error, although leakage-consistent readout events were clearly detected. A daily automated study is now running, with three claims fixed in advance.

2. Prior work and what is not new

Reset elimination on heavy-hex was published first by Harper et al., arXiv:2504.07258, Nature Communications 17, 5039 (2026): ibm_marrakesh, d = 3, 96–97% per-round fidelity with a shorter circuit. Reset-free extraction was analysed in simulation by Gehér et al. (arXiv:2408.00758). IBM's 2022 heavy-hex experiment is arXiv:2203.07205. Soft-information decoding on IBM hardware: arXiv:2411.16228. Noise-aware decoding: arXiv:2502.21044, 2406.02700. Leakage detection with analog readout: Varbanov et al., npj QI 2020. This work is an independent replication on another chip with IBM's original flag-qubit circuit, plus the tests listed above. Novelty claims rest on targeted searches, not a full review.

4. Fix log (everything that was corrected)

Report corrections (14 Oct → 15 Oct)

  • Eight pre-registered predictions that had failed are now marked FAILS (P3, P4, P6, P10, P13, P15, P16, P19). Some had been shown as "Mixed" or not mentioned.
  • The 14 Oct break-even "pass" was obtained after excluding a dead qubit (q146) post hoc. It is now labelled that way and was retested cleanly with a dead-qubit filter applied before selection.
  • Dynamical-decoupling table: the arms without DD were saturated (45–50% error after 3 rounds), so the ×1.21–1.27 magnitude was withdrawn. The per-round metric there differed from the rest of the report.
  • Added shot counts and sources for every number. The "Λ < 1 on hardware" claim was withdrawn (never run at d = 5).

Independent code and physics reviews

  • The re-derivation script merged two DD arm types; fixed. All 8 arms then reproduce, including P6 = 7.48%.
  • Break-even lifetime model: a review concern (2.6× error) was checked against the exact DD pulse-sequence model. The original fit was within about 5%, and the Z ratio was corrected from 0.096× to 0.092×.
  • The probe-model prediction had been decoded with its own noise model instead of the calibration decoder used on the hardware data. This was fixed for all future runs, and the 15 Oct result is caveated.
  • Daily pipeline: no same-day resubmission; plan saved before submission; budget guard fails closed; per-day error records; results always committed. The protocol was bumped to v2 before any daily result.

False positives caught by simulation before hardware

  • Noise spectroscopy: a "≥ 60% of qubits" rule passed on pure white noise. It was replaced with a sign test plus effect size, giving 0/20 false positives.
  • A Ramsey measured only in X gave 10/10 false positives under static frequency offsets. It is now X+Y magnitude with per-qubit readout correction, giving 0/20 with confounds (offsets, ZZ, asymmetric readout).
  • Leakage decoding: readout-only erasure hurts once leakage spreads to neighbours, so the neighbour-aware decoder was built. False-flag cost was quantified, and a binomial persistence test now gates the flags.

Audit of earlier exploratory notebooks

31 earlier simulation notebooks were audited, fixed and re-run. Several headline effects turned out to be implementation bugs: a reservoir that reset every step; an 8-bit integer overflow; a non-Hermitian Hamiltonian; integrator norm drift; and hard-coded "CONFIRMED" strings. With the bugs fixed, those claims were withdrawn. Correct reproductions of known physics remained, for example the toric-code threshold at 10.38% against about 10.3% in the literature. These notebooks are not published.

5. Running now: pre-registered daily study

A GitHub Action runs every day on IBM hardware, alternating ibm_kingston and ibm_marrakesh. It probes all qubits, runs baseline and reset-free arms, and publishes daily/RESULTS.md. Claims fixed before any data:

  • C1: the calibration-fed model is at least 2× less accurate than the probe-fed model on at least 80% of days.
  • C2: the reset-free Z gain is 2σ-separated on at least 80% of days.
  • C3: detector excess tracks readout drift on at least 60% of days.

No verdict before 20 analysed days.

6. Limitations and next steps

  • Distance 3 only. A matching decoder only. Few days per chip. IBM open-plan budget: 600 s of QPU per month.
  • The leakage events could not be calibrated (|2⟩ cannot be prepared on Heron), so no leakage rate is claimed.
  • Next: let the daily study reach 20 days. If more QPU time becomes available, test whether leakage accumulates over many rounds (pre-registered), and run noise spectroscopy on hardware.

All claims are traceable to files in the repository. Simulations are labelled as such and say nothing about hardware.
